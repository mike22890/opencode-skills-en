# Influence & Communication

## Speaking to specific types

| Type | Don't say | Do say |
|---|---|---|
| Anxious | "Don't overthink" (invalidating) | "I'm here, I'm not leaving" (stable) + concrete commitment |
| Avoidant | "Why won't you talk to me" (chasing) | "Talk when you're ready" (space) + don't chase |
| Narcissistic | Direct criticism (triggers attack) | Give face + frame in their interest ("this benefits you") |
| Highly defensive | "You're wrong" (triggers defense) | "I might be misunderstanding, help me see" (soft entry) |
| People-pleaser | Accepting over-giving (reinforcing) | "You don't have to do this for me" (release) + thanks |
| Dominant | Head-on clash (escalates) | Facts + boundary ("this is the line") |

## Deep listening (Voss upgraded)

**Three steps**:
1. **Mirror**: repeat their last 2-3 words ("a lot of pressure?")
2. **Label**: "Sounds like you feel..." (naming the emotion)
3. **Calibrated question**: "How can we..." / "What made you..." (makes them think)

**Banned**: "why" (triggers defense) — replace with "what made you..."

## Emotional processing (join first, lead later)

**Wrong**: their anger → you lecture (rational brain offline, nothing lands)

**Right**:
1. **Join**: catch the emotion ("I'd be angry too")
2. **Wait**: emotion drops (~20 minutes)
3. **Lead**: then discuss solutions ("what do you want to do now?")

**Affect labeling**: naming the emotion itself reduces intensity — "you feel wronged right now, don't you".

## Boundary setting (three-part method)

```
1. Affirm the relationship: "I care about you"
2. State the boundary: "But I can't accept this"
3. Offer a choice: "If it continues, I will leave"
```

**Points**:
- **Specific** (not "you never respect me", but "you yelled at me in front of others")
- **Firm** (don't over-explain — explaining = negotiating room)
- **Enforced** (say but don't do = boundary bankruptcy)

## Questioning techniques

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Open | Gather info | "What was that like?" |
| Calibrated | Guide thinking | "How could this be solved?" |
| Socratic | Dismantle logic | "If that were true, what follows?" |
| Miracle question | Bypass defenses to find goals | "If the problem vanished tomorrow, what would you do first?" |
| Scaling | Quantify vague feelings | "1-10, where are you now?" |

## Psychological order of persuasion

```
1. Build safety (nervous system must relax to hear)
2. Agree partially ("you're right, and..." not "but")
3. Offer a new angle (don't negate the old, add new)
4. Let them say the conclusion (people believe what they say)
```

**Rule**: people don't change because "you're right" — only because "I feel safe + this is my own idea".

## Quick card

```
Types: anxious→stability / avoidant→space / narcissist→benefit / defensive→soft entry

Persuasion: safety → agreement → new angle → their own conclusion
```
